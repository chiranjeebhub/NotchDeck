A working replica. Hover the notch to open it and move away to tuck it in, or drop a file from your Mac onto the screen to put it in the Tray. The orange dot means Claude is waiting on you: answer it in Dev → Agents.

Features

Everything, one hover away.

Built in Swift and SwiftUI, so it feels like part of macOS. It lives in the menu bar with no Dock icon, and the notch looks like a notch until you need it.

Now Playing, for any app

Artwork, a seekable progress bar and play, pause and skip for whatever macOS is playing: Spotify, Music, Podcasts or YouTube in a browser. The closed notch shows the album art and a live level meter.

Calendar, day or month

Today’s meetings at a glance, or a month view with dots on busy days. One-click Join for Google Meet, Zoom, Teams and Webex, for every account in the Calendar app.

A tray for your files

Drop files on the notch to keep them handy, then drag them out wherever they’re going. Quick Look thumbnails, an AirDrop tile, and the tray is still there after a restart.

A Dev tab for builders

Live CPU, memory and disk, every server listening on your Mac with its project folder, Docker containers and top processes. Open, copy or stop anything in a click.

Your AI agents, at a glance

Every Claude Code and Codex session and what it’s doing. When Claude needs permission the notch lights up, and you can Allow or Deny without hunting for the right terminal tab.

Make it yours

Hide or reorder tabs, pick the widgets on Home and the three Dev stats, choose calendars, and open on hover or click. Notes live in the notch too, and updates install themselves.

Setup

Ready in about a minute.

There’s no account and nothing to configure before it’s useful. Everything after the second step is optional.

  1. Install itDrag it to Applications or use Homebrew. It opens without warnings because it’s notarized.
  2. Hover the notchMusic, calendar and the Tray work straight away. Calendar access is asked for the first time it’s needed.
  3. Connect Claude CodeOne click in Settings → Dev, and agent sessions and permission prompts show up in the notch.
  4. Turn on long commandsAdd the one-line shell integration for zsh, bash or fish from the same page.
Privacy

It all stays on your Mac.

NotchDeck shows you what’s already on your Mac. There’s no NotchDeck server, so there’s nowhere for your data to go.

Questions

Good to know.

Something else? Open an issue on GitHub.

Is NotchDeck really free?

Yes. It’s free and MIT licensed, with no subscription, account or in-app purchase.

My Mac doesn’t have a notch. Does it still work?

Yes. Displays without a notch, including external monitors, get a slim handle at the top of the screen that opens the same deck.

Will it get in the way?

It looks like a normal notch until you hover it, and tucks back in when you move away. If hovering feels too eager, set it to open on click instead, or lengthen the hover delay.

How does it see my Claude Code sessions?

Click Connect in Settings → Dev and NotchDeck adds a small hook to Claude Code that reports what each session is doing and passes permission prompts to the notch. Codex sessions are picked up from the files Codex keeps on your Mac.

Why isn’t it on the Mac App Store?

The App Store sandbox doesn’t allow system-wide Now Playing or looking at the servers and processes on your Mac, which is most of the Dev tab. The direct download is signed and notarized by Apple instead.
